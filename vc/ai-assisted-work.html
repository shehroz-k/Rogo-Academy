Quality & usage: stop the slop, keep the speed

How investment teams use AI to move faster without lowering the bar: a working standard, the failure modes to catch, six workflows that compound and a QC pass to run before anything reaches a partner.

Working standard

AI can assist. You own the answer.

Use Felix to get to a strong first draft faster. Then check the evidence, challenge the logic and own what you send to a partner or investment committee. The reviewer should never be the first person to verify a number.

01

Treat it as a starting point

A first draft, not the finished product and not the final word.

02

Verify before you rely

Check every number, quote and claim that matters against the original source.

03

Keep judgment in the loop

You decide what matters, what is missing and what the evidence supports.

04

Own what you submit

You can explain every assumption without asking AI again.

05

Say what you did not check

State sources, as-of dates and open items explicitly.

06

Follow firm policy

Approved tools and data only. AI supports review; it does not replace it.

Four checkpoints

01 · Before the first prompt

Define the task

Name the sources, data vintage, scope and the output format the partner expects.

02 · Before trusting the output

Check the work

Trace sources, dates, units and definitions. Recalculate derived figures.

03 · Before anyone reviews it

Explain the answer

State the drivers, the downside case and the open issues.

04 · Before it leaves your desk

Submit with a record

Document the checks you ran and escalate material issues first.

Quality

What AI slop looks like in venture work

Slop is output that looks finished but has not earned trust: polished, confident and wrong in ways a partner catches in seconds. These are the failure modes to hunt for.

Failure modeWhat it looks likeThe check
Stale or invented financing dataWrong round, date, lead or amount; a company that has since been acquired or shut downTrace to a primary source (press release, filing, data provider record) and note the as-of date
Entity mix-upsTwo companies with similar names merged into one profileConfirm domain, HQ, founders and product before using any fact
Metric conflationARR presented as GAAP revenue; bookings presented as ARRConfirm the metric, its definition and the period
Company-defined metricsNRR that excludes small customers; "ARR" that includes services or signed-not-live contractsAsk for the definition; recompute on a consistent basis
Market size inflationPayment volume or total spend presented as software TAM; trillions vs. billionsRebuild bottom-up: target accounts × ACV
Synthesis beyond the evidence"No direct competitors," "best-in-class," "category leader" with no supportSeparate fact from inference; ask for contrary evidence
Arithmetic in derived figuresBurn multiple inverted, growth on the wrong base, ownership math offRecalculate from the underlying inputs yourself
Citation that does not prove the claimA real source that says something adjacent, or at another dateOpen the passage: does it say exactly this?
Generic filler"Poised to disrupt a massive, underserved market"Delete it or replace it with a specific, checkable claim
Confidential data handlingData room or LP material pasted into unapproved toolsUse approved tools only and follow firm policy

Drill: review the draft against the source

Company snapshot: Cobalt Ledger

AI-generated first draft · prepared for partner meeting prep · unreviewed

Cobalt Ledger sells accounts payable automation software to mid-market finance teams. ARR grew 140% year over year, from $5.0M to $12.0M. On LTM revenue of $12.0M, the proposed $200M post-money implies 16.7x trailing revenue. Net revenue retention of 118% is best-in-class for the category. The company closed a $40M Series B led by Halcyon Ventures in March 2025. Net burn of $14.0M against $7.0M of net new ARR gives a highly efficient burn multiple of 0.5x. The top 10 customers account for 31% of ARR across a base of 310 customers. Cobalt Ledger addresses a $4.2 trillion market. Competitors such as Paylane and Invoicely do not serve the mid-market, leaving Cobalt Ledger without direct competition. CEO Dana Reyes was previously VP Finance Operations at a mid-market ERP company that was acquired in 2021. With its innovative AI-powered platform, Cobalt Ledger is poised to disrupt a massive, underserved market. Gross revenue retention was 88%.

Data room extract (verified)

  • ARR: $12.0M at Sep-2026; $5.0M at Sep-2025
  • LTM GAAP revenue: $9.1M
  • LTM net burn: $14.0M; net new ARR: $7.0M
  • Retention (company definition, excludes customers under $10K ARR): NRR 118%, GRR 88%
  • Customers: 310; top 10 = 31% of ARR
  • Financing: Seed $3M (2022); Series A $15M (June 2024, led by Halcyon Ventures). Series B in process, not closed.
  • CEO Dana Reyes: previously VP Finance Operations at a mid-market ERP company, acquired in 2021
  • Market: a third-party report estimates 2025 mid-market AP automation software spend at $4.2B
  • Competitors in the management deck: Paylane (mid-market focus), Invoicely (SMB to mid-market)

What a reviewer should catch

"On LTM revenue of $12.0M, the proposed $200M post-money implies 16.7x trailing revenue."
Metric conflation $12.0M is ARR at Sep-2026. LTM GAAP revenue is $9.1M, so the trailing revenue multiple is 22.0x; 16.7x is the multiple on ARR. Label the metric and the period.
"Net revenue retention of 118% is best-in-class for the category."
Unsupported characterization 118% is the company's own definition, which excludes customers under $10K ARR. On all customers the figure is 116% (see Module 3). "Best-in-class" needs a benchmark the source does not provide.
"The company closed a $40M Series B led by Halcyon Ventures in March 2025."
Stale or invented financing data Halcyon Ventures led the $15M Series A in June 2024. The Series B is still in process and has not closed. The draft invented a round. Trace financing history to a primary source.
"Net burn of $14.0M against $7.0M of net new ARR gives a highly efficient burn multiple of 0.5x."
Arithmetic in a derived figure Burn multiple = net burn ÷ net new ARR = 14.0 ÷ 7.0 = 2.0x. The draft inverted the ratio, then drew the opposite conclusion. By common rules of thumb, 2.0x is expensive growth.
"Cobalt Ledger addresses a $4.2 trillion market."
Units The third-party estimate is $4.2B of mid-market AP automation software spend. Trillions usually means total payment volume, which is not a software market.
"Competitors such as Paylane and Invoicely do not serve the mid-market, leaving Cobalt Ledger without direct competition."
Contradicts the source The management deck names Paylane as mid-market focused and Invoicely as SMB to mid-market. The claim contradicts the source, and "no direct competition" is almost never a defensible statement.
"With its innovative AI-powered platform, Cobalt Ledger is poised to disrupt a massive, underserved market."
Generic filler No checkable claim. The source says nothing about AI. Replace it with a specific statement: who buys, what changes for them and the evidence.

The five unhighlighted sentences are supported by the source. Flagging them costs reviewer time too, so precision matters as much as recall.

Usage

Using AI where it compounds

Six recurring investment-team workflows. Each comes with a prompt starter written to produce checkable output, the verification it needs and where it usually goes wrong. Save the prompts you refine as reusable skills, so the whole team runs the same standard.

Where it helps

Sourcing screens

Build a first-pass target list from a thesis in minutes, then spend your time on verification and outreach.

Verify before you rely
  • The company exists at that domain and is still independent
  • Last round, date and lead against a primary source
  • Stage and check-size fit for your fund
  • Similar-name collisions and duplicates
  • Whether it is already in your pipeline or CRM
Where it goes wrong

Stale rounds, acquired companies, merged entities and confident "fit" scores built on thin evidence.

Prompt starterEdit the brackets
Build a list of up to 25 US companies that sell [workflow] software to [customer segment], founded after [year], showing [signal, e.g. growth in customer-facing hiring].
For each company give: website, HQ, founders, last disclosed round (amount, date, lead) with a source and as-of date, a one-line product description and why it fits this thesis: [paste thesis].
Mark any field you could not verify as "unverified" rather than estimating it. Exclude companies that have been acquired or shut down. Sort by thesis fit, and explain the ranking in one line each.
Where it helps

Market maps

Get a first structure for a landscape, then pressure-test segmentation and placements with your team.

Verify before you rely
  • The segmentation logic matches how customers actually buy
  • Each company placement against its own website or materials
  • Missing players (ask your team; AI lists skew to well-covered names)
  • Whether "white space" reflects a real market
Where it goes wrong

Lists biased toward heavily covered companies, and empty quadrants read as opportunity when the market may not exist.

Prompt starterEdit the brackets
Map the [category] landscape for [customer segment]. Segment by customer size and core workflow. In each segment, list incumbents and venture-backed challengers with a one-line positioning statement and a source.
Identify segments with few venture-backed players and explain whether that looks like white space or a weak market.
Separate sourced facts from your inferences, and list three developments that would change this map.
Where it helps

Founder & meeting prep

Walk into a first meeting with verified context and questions that test your thesis risk.

Verify before you rely
  • Career dates and titles against the person's own profile
  • That you have the right person (same-name confusion is common)
  • The recency and source of each news item
Where it goes wrong

The wrong person, inflated titles and old news presented as current.

Prompt starterEdit the brackets
Prepare a one-page brief for my first meeting with [founder], CEO of [company].
Include: verified career history (roles, dates and sources), company facts with as-of dates, news from the last 12 months, and five questions that test [our key thesis risk].
Flag anything you could not verify. Do not speculate about personal matters.
Where it helps

First-draft memo sections

Draft from your data room and notes so your time goes into judgment, not formatting.

Verify before you rely
  • Every figure against the cited page
  • Metric definitions (ARR vs. revenue, NRR basis)
  • That conclusions are no stronger than the evidence
  • That the thesis is in your own voice and you can defend it
Where it goes wrong

Management claims restated as facts, and confident conclusions stitched together from partial support.

Prompt starterEdit the brackets
Using only the attached data room materials and my notes, draft the [traction / market / risks] section of an investment memo for [company].
Keep management claims, verified data and our underwriting assumptions separate and labeled. Cite the document and page for every figure.
End with a list of open diligence questions. Do not add market data I did not supply.
Where it helps

Comps & transactions

Assemble trading comps and private financing data quickly, then check dates, definitions and structure.

Verify before you rely
  • All multiples are on the same date and basis (NTM vs. LTM)
  • Reported vs. rumored valuations
  • Structure behind headline valuations (preferences, ratchets)
  • Currency and units
Where it goes wrong

Mixed dates, LTM and NTM blended in one column, and headline valuations treated as clean prices.

Prompt starterEdit the brackets
Pull trading data for [peer list] as of [date]: enterprise value, EV / NTM revenue, NTM revenue growth and gross margin, with sources.
Then list disclosed private financings in [category] since [date]: company, date, amount, lead investor, post-money valuation if disclosed, and source.
Mark undisclosed values as undisclosed. Do not estimate.
Where it helps

Portfolio & LP updates

Turn board decks and KPI files into consistent quarterly updates.

Verify before you rely
  • KPIs tie to the source file
  • Definitions are consistent from quarter to quarter
  • Confidentiality of portfolio and LP materials
Where it goes wrong

Silent definition changes, and management optimism presented as the firm's view.

Prompt starterEdit the brackets
Draft the quarterly update for [portfolio company] from the attached board deck and KPI file.
Report KPIs exactly as defined in the KPI file, show the change from last quarter, and flag any metric whose definition changed.
Keep commentary factual, and label forward-looking statements as management expectations.
Quality control

A structured QC pass before partner review

Run one of these prompts in Felix with your draft and its sources. Felix returns located, ranked findings. You verify and resolve them, then the work goes to human review.

1 · You

Prepare & self-check

Draft, sources and the standard to review against.

2 · Felix

First-pass review

Located findings, evidence and what it could not check.

3 · You

Verify & resolve

Correct or defend each finding against the source.

4 · Partner / IC

Independent review

Challenge and approval under the firm's process.

Investment memo QCMemos & IC decks
Act as a demanding partner reviewing this investment memo before investment committee. Be tough on the work, not the person.
First confirm the stage, round terms, audience and the source materials supplied. Ask if anything is missing.
Then:
1. Trace every material figure to the supplied sources. Flag any figure without a source, with a stale as-of date, or with a definition that differs from the source (for example ARR vs. revenue, or company-defined NRR).
2. Recalculate derived figures: growth, retention, burn multiple, CAC payback, ownership, MOIC and IRR.
3. Test whether each conclusion is supported by the evidence. Separate management claims, verified data and our assumptions.
4. Check valuation and returns: entry price, dilution to exit, required exit value and scenario weighting.
5. Name the strongest contrary evidence and any thesis falsifiers the memo omits.
6. Flag generic language that makes no checkable claim.
Return: My read (three sentences); Fix before IC (located and ranked); Be ready to defend; Three questions I would ask live; What I could not verify.
Do not rewrite the memo, invent defects or citations, or approve the investment. "No issue found" is not assurance.
Returns & cap table model QCModels
Review the attached returns or cap table model as a senior investor before investment committee.
First confirm the round terms, share counts and model version.
Then:
1. Tie the cap table to the term sheet: pre-money, investment, price per share, option pool size and whether it sits in the pre-money.
2. Check fully diluted share counts, including options, warrants and any SAFEs or notes.
3. Recalculate ownership at entry and after each modeled round.
4. Test the exit waterfall at a low, middle and high exit value, including liquidation preference and conversion.
5. Recompute MOIC and IRR from dated cash flows.
6. Look for hardcodes, broken links, sign errors and unit mismatches.
Return located findings (sheet and cell), materiality and the fix, then list what you could not verify. Do not silently correct the model.
Sourcing list QCTarget lists & market maps
Review this AI-generated target list before any outreach.
For each row:
1. Confirm the company exists at the stated domain and is still independent and operating.
2. Check the last round, date and lead against a primary source, and note the as-of date.
3. Flag rows that may confuse two similarly named companies.
4. Test the stated thesis fit against this thesis: [paste thesis].
5. Flag duplicates, and companies already in our pipeline if I attach the export.
Return: rows to remove (with the reason), rows to correct, rows verified, and fields you could not verify.
RememberA second AI pass can miss errors too. A clean QC report is not assurance.
Before you hit send

Pre-submission checks

A polished draft is not evidence of accuracy.

Practice check

Five questions

Question 1 of 5
An AI-drafted snapshot says "ARR of $12.0M" in one paragraph and "LTM revenue of $12.0M" in the next. What do you do?
They are different metrics and are rarely equal for a fast-growing company. Cobalt Ledger has $12.0M of ARR but $9.1M of LTM revenue.
Question 2 of 5
The output cites a source for a funding round. What is the right check?
A citation that says something adjacent, such as an earlier round or a different company, is a finding. Asking the same tool to confirm itself is not verification.
Question 3 of 5
Which prompt produces the most checkable output?
Specific scope, named fields, sources and dates, and an instruction to mark gaps make the output verifiable row by row.
Question 4 of 5
A Felix QC pass on your memo returns "no material issues found." What does that mean?
A second AI pass can miss errors too. QC is the start of review, not assurance.
Question 5 of 5
Where is AI output most likely to be stale in venture work?
Private-market facts change constantly and are unevenly reported. Treat every round, valuation and status as unverified until traced to a primary source with a date.

Graded case in Felix: Spot the Slop

Paste this prompt into a new Felix chat. Felix builds a fresh source pack and a flawed draft, withholds the answers, grades your review and retests you on a new case.

Tutor promptAbout 30 minutes
You are my Rogo Academy tutor for "AI-Assisted Work: Spot the Slop" (Venture & Growth Equity track). Teach like a demanding but fair senior investor.

Rules for this session:
1. Build a fresh fictional case: a fictional venture-backed company with a source pack of 10 verified facts (ARR, revenue, burn, retention with its definition, customers and concentration, financing history, founder background, market size with units, named competitors), plus an AI-drafted 12-sentence company snapshot containing 5 to 7 planted errors across different failure modes (metric conflation, stale or invented financing, arithmetic, units, unsupported characterization, contradiction of the source, generic filler). Do not tell me how many errors there are. Use realistic but invented names and numbers. Do not reuse Cobalt Ledger or any real company.
2. Give me the case inputs and the task only. Do not show the answer, the completed calculations or a worked solution until I submit my own work.
3. If I ask for the answer before submitting, give me one targeted hint at a time instead.
4. My task: Return a located list of every problem (quote the sentence, name the failure mode, give the correction with the source fact), then rewrite the snapshot as one clean paragraph that is specific, sourced and free of filler, and list what remains unverified.
5. When I submit, grade my work out of 100 against this rubric:
  - Detection: share of planted errors found, net of false flags (40)
  - Correction accuracy against the source pack (25)
  - Failure mode correctly named (10)
  - Rewritten paragraph is specific, sourced and free of filler (15)
  - States what remains unverified (10)
   For each criterion show my score, what I got right, each error (where it is and why it matters) and the corrected approach.
6. Then ask me two explain-back questions on the key drivers, without AI help, and grade my answers.
7. Finish with a retest case that changes the numbers and one structural feature. Same rules: no answers until I submit.
Keep the whole exercise to about 30 minutes. Start by confirming my experience level in one question, then present the case.
