VC Math: Ownership, Dilution & Returns

Round math, dilution to exit, MOIC and IRR, required exit value and fund-returner math.

Teach

Round math

Post-money = Pre-money + New investment Investor stake = Investment ÷ Post-money Price per share = Pre-money ÷ Pre-money fully diluted shares New shares = Investment ÷ Price per share

Dilution to exit

Every later round and option pool top-up dilutes existing holders. If a holder does not buy more, ownership falls by the share of the company each round sells:

Exit ownership = Entry ownership × (1 − d₁) × (1 − d₂) × … × (1 − dₙ)

Pro rata rights let an investor buy enough of the next round to hold its stake, but that takes reserve capital. Whether to exercise pro rata is a portfolio decision, covered in Module 8.

Returns

MOIC = Proceeds ÷ Invested capital IRR = MOIC^(1 ÷ years) − 1 (single investment, single exit)

The same 3x is excellent over three years (44% IRR) and mediocre over ten (12%). Funds report both, and LPs care about both.

Working backward: the exit you need

Required exit equity value = Target proceeds ÷ Exit ownership Fund-returner exit = Fund size ÷ Exit ownership

Venture returns follow a power law: a few outcomes drive most of a fund's return. So the key question for every initial check is whether this company could plausibly return the fund at the ownership you will have at exit, not whether the round is fairly priced.

Worked example · Cobalt Ledger

Entry. $40M Series B at a $160M pre-money gives a $200M post-money and 20.0% ownership.

Dilution. Assume a Series C sells 15%, a Series D sells 12% and option refreshes take 3%. Retention = 0.85 × 0.88 × 0.97 = 72.6%. Exit ownership = 20.0% × 72.6% = 14.5%.

Required exit. For 5x on $40M ($200M of proceeds), the company must exit at $200M ÷ 14.5% = $1.38B. To return a $750M fund from this one position, it would need to exit at $5.17B.

Returns. If the company exits at $1.5B in year 6, proceeds are 14.5% × $1.5B = $217.7M: 5.4x and about a 33% IRR.

The judgment. Is a $1.4B outcome plausible for mid-market AP automation? Is $5B? The math does not answer that. It tells you exactly what you have to believe.

Practice check

Five questions

Question 1 of 5
An investor puts $8M into a company at a $32M pre-money. What does it own?
Post-money = $40M; $8M ÷ $40M = 20%.
Question 2 of 5
You own 20% after the Series A. The next two rounds each sell 20% and 15%, and you take no pro rata. What do you own?
20% × 0.80 × 0.85 = 13.6%.
Question 3 of 5
What is the IRR of a 3x return over 5 years, with one investment and one exit?
3^(1/5) − 1 ≈ 24.6%.
Question 4 of 5
Exit ownership will be 10% and you need $300M of proceeds. What exit value do you need?
$300M ÷ 10% = $3.0B.
Question 5 of 5
Why can a 3x MOIC be a great or a mediocre outcome?
3x over 3 years is about a 44% IRR; over 10 years it is about 12%. A 3x on a tiny check may not move a large fund.

AI watch-outs for this topic

Where AI output goes wrong here

  • Ownership math on the pre-money instead of the post-money.
  • Ignoring dilution from future rounds and pool refreshes when computing required exits.
  • IRR computed as MOIC divided by years, which is wrong.
  • Mixing equity value and enterprise value in exit math.
  • Fund-returner math on fund size vs. investable capital without saying which (see Module 8).
