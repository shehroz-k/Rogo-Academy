SaaS Metrics & Unit Economics

ARR bridge, NRR and GRR, CAC payback, burn multiple — and how company definitions flatter them.

Teach

The ARR bridge

Ending ARR = Beginning ARR + New logo ARR + Expansion − Contraction − Churn Net new ARR = Ending ARR − Beginning ARR

Always ask for the bridge, not just the ending number. Two companies with the same growth can have very different businesses underneath.

Retention

NRR (net revenue retention) = (Beginning + Expansion − Contraction − Churn) ÷ Beginning GRR (gross revenue retention) = (Beginning − Contraction − Churn) ÷ Beginning

NRR shows whether the existing base grows on its own. GRR, which caps at 100%, shows how much leaks out. High NRR with low GRR means expansion is masking churn. Check the basis: cohort or period, all customers or a subset, and dollar or logo retention.

Customer acquisition efficiency

CAC payback (months) = S&M spend ÷ (New + Expansion ARR × Gross margin) × 12 Magic number = Net new ARR ÷ S&M spend (annual simplification) Burn multiple = Net burn ÷ Net new ARR Rule of 40 = Revenue growth % + FCF margin %

Use a gross-margin-adjusted payback: revenue is not the cash available to recover acquisition cost. Prefer payback to LTV/CAC early on, because LTV depends on long-dated churn assumptions you cannot observe yet.

MetricCommonly cited heuristicCaveat
Burn multipleUnder 1x excellent; 1–1.5x good; 1.5–2x needs a plan; over 2x expensiveEarly-stage companies run higher; the trend matters
CAC paybackUnder 12 months strong for SMB; under 18–24 months acceptable for mid-marketDepends on contract length and gross margin

Treat these as conversation starters, not pass/fail lines. The question is always why the number is what it is, and whether it is improving.

Churn(0.4)
Ending ARR (Sep-2026)12.0
  • NRR = (5.0 + 1.4 − 0.2 − 0.4) ÷ 5.0 = 116% on all customers. The company reports 118% because it excludes customers under $10K ARR. Use the all-customer figure, or disclose the definition every time you quote 118%.
  • GRR = (5.0 − 0.2 − 0.4) ÷ 5.0 = 88%.
  • Burn multiple = 14.0 ÷ 7.0 = 2.0x: expensive growth. The Series B plan should show a path to below 1.5x.
  • CAC payback = 11.0 ÷ ((6.2 + 1.4) × 78%) × 12 = about 22 months, acceptable for mid-market but worth pressure-testing by channel.
  • Magic number = 7.0 ÷ 11.0 = 0.64.
  • Rule of 40 = 140% growth + (−154%) FCF margin ≈ −14. Not meaningful at $12M ARR. Do not let anyone put it on the front page.

Practice check

Five questions

Question 1 of 5
Beginning ARR $10M; expansion $2M; contraction $0.5M; churn $1M. What are NRR and GRR?
NRR = (10 + 2 − 0.5 − 1) ÷ 10 = 105%. GRR = (10 − 0.5 − 1) ÷ 10 = 85%.
Question 2 of 5
A company reports 130% NRR and 75% GRR. What is the best read?
A quarter of the base leaks every year. If expansion slows, growth falls fast. Ask for logo and dollar cohorts.
Question 3 of 5
Net burn $20M; net new ARR $8M. What is the burn multiple?
20 ÷ 8 = 2.5x: each dollar of new ARR costs $2.50 of burn.
Question 4 of 5
Why adjust CAC payback for gross margin?
A $100K customer at 50% gross margin recovers CAC half as fast as one at 100%.
Question 5 of 5
A company's NRR excludes customers under $10K ARR. What should you do?
Excluding small customers usually removes the highest-churn segment. Compare like for like across companies.

AI watch-outs for this topic

Where AI output goes wrong here

  • Quoting company-defined NRR or ARR as if it were standard. Always state the definition.
  • Mixing annual and quarterly figures in one ratio, for example quarterly S&M against annual net new ARR.
  • Burn multiple or payback inverted, or computed on revenue instead of ARR.
  • Benchmarks stated as facts without stage or segment context.
  • LTV figures built on an assumed churn rate the data cannot support.
