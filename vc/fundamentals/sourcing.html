Teach

Why sourcing is the edge

Diligence inputs such as financials, market data and expert calls are increasingly accessible to every firm, and AI makes them faster to assemble. What still separates investors is seeing the right company earlier, with conviction already formed, and earning the founder's time. Sourcing is a system, not a lucky inbound.

Universe → Screened list → Contacted → First meeting → Deep dive → IC → Term sheet

Each stage narrows sharply. Most firms see hundreds or thousands of companies for every investment, so small gains at the top of the funnel and in conversion compound.

Thesis-driven vs. reactive sourcing

Reactive sourcing waits for decks. Thesis-driven sourcing starts with a view about change and works backward to the companies that should exist. A good thesis is specific enough to be proved wrong:

Because [structural change], [customer] will [new behavior], creating room for [type of company]. Winners will have [2–3 characteristics]. We would be wrong if [observable falsifier].

Market maps

Segment a landscape by customer (who buys, at what size), workflow (what job the product does) and business model (seat, usage or transaction). Then place incumbents and challengers. Ask why incumbents cannot easily serve a segment; structural constraints such as channel conflict, legacy architecture or pricing models matter more than feature gaps.

Signals: what to watch and how much to trust it

SignalWhat it can indicateWatch out for
Hiring velocity and mixFunded growth; customer-facing hires imply revenue tractionHiring announced ahead of revenue; contractors not visible
Named customers and case studiesReal deployments in your target segmentPilots dressed up as customers; logos from free tiers
Product cadenceA team that ships; changelog and release notesCosmetic releases
Web, app or community tractionDemand and engagement trendsPaid traffic spikes; bots; wrong-geography traffic
Founder backgroundEarned insight into the problemPedigree is not evidence of fit
Pricing page changesMoving upmarket, packaging maturityPrice tests that do not stick
Regulatory or platform shiftsA new reason for a company to exist nowTiming: rules announced long before they bite

No single signal is enough. Triangulate: two or three independent signals pointing the same way are worth more than one dramatic one.

Pipeline hygiene

Every company in the pipeline needs a stage, an owner, a next step with a date, the last touch and, when you pass, the reason you passed. Pass reasons let you re-engage when the reason changes ("too early" → "now at $5M ARR") and show where your thesis is miscalibrated.

The first touch

Short, specific and earned. Show you did the work, give a reason to talk now and make a clear ask.

Subject: [Specific observation], [firm] thesis on [category] Line 1: what you noticed (a customer, launch, hire or post) and why it matters. Line 2: the thesis you are working on and the one insight you can bring. Line 3: a clear, small ask — 25 minutes next week.
Worked example · Cobalt Ledger

Thesis. Because mid-market finance teams are being asked to close faster with flat headcount, and because e-invoicing mandates are spreading, AP teams will replace manual invoice processing with automated software built for their size. The winners will offer fast implementation (weeks, not quarters), native ERP integrations and payment monetization. We would be wrong if mid-market buyers keep choosing ERP-bundled modules or spend management suites add AP as a free feature.

Map. Segment by company size (SMB, mid-market, enterprise) and workflow (invoice capture, approvals, payments, vendor management). Enterprise suites dominate the top end and SMB tools the low end. The mid-market AP segment has few venture-backed specialists.

Signal. Cobalt Ledger doubles its account executive headcount over two quarters and publishes four mid-market case studies naming ERP integrations. Two independent, revenue-linked signals point the same way.

Score. Market 4, Team 4, Traction 4, Thesis fit 5, Timing 4 (raising in the next six months), Access 3 (a portfolio CFO is a customer). With the default weights, it scores 4.10: Prioritize. The portfolio CFO is the warm route in.

Practice check

Five questions

Question 1 of 5
Taken alone, which is the strongest early signal for a B2B software company?
Two independent signals tied to revenue (sales hiring and named deployments) beat attention signals, which are cheap to buy.
Question 2 of 5
What makes a sourcing thesis useful?
A falsifiable thesis tells you which companies should exist, what to look for and when to walk away.
Question 3 of 5
Felix returns a list of 40 target companies for your thesis. What is the first step before outreach?
Lists drift: acquired companies, stale rounds and merged entities are common. Verify before your name goes on an email.
Question 4 of 5
Why record a pass reason for every company?
"Too early" or "no enterprise traction" are conditions that change. Pass reasons turn the pipeline into a resurfacing engine.
Question 5 of 5
Which first line of a cold email is strongest?
Specific, shows homework and offers a relevant reason to talk. The firm description can come later.

AI watch-outs for this topic

Where AI output goes wrong here

  • Hallucinated or stale companies: acquired, shut down or never real. Ask for a source and an as-of date for every row.
  • Wrong stage or funding data, especially rounds from the last 12 months.
  • Merged entities when two companies share a similar name. Confirm the domain and founders.
  • Market sizes with no source, or payment volume presented as a software market.
  • Generic descriptors such as "AI-powered platform" that tell you nothing about fit. Ask for the specific workflow and customer.
