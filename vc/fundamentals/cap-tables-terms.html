Fully diluted shares

Fully diluted (FD) shares include common, preferred on an as-converted basis, granted options, the unallocated option pool, warrants and, where the term sheet says so, SAFEs and notes. Price per share is the pre-money divided by pre-money FD shares, so what goes into that count matters.

The option pool shuffle

Investors often require a larger pool, sized as a percentage of the post-money, but created in the pre-money. The new pool shares dilute only existing holders. The headline pre-money stays the same, but the price per share falls.

Effective pre-money = Headline pre-money − Value of the new pool shares

SAFEs and convertible notes

A post-money SAFE converts into a fixed ownership before the priced round's new money: investment ÷ post-money cap. A $2M SAFE at a $10M post-money cap converts to 20%, which is then diluted by the priced round. Discounts give the SAFE holder the lower of the cap price and a discount to the round price. Convertible notes work similarly but accrue interest and have a maturity date.

Liquidation preferences

TypeIn a sale, the investor receivesEffect
1x non-participatingThe greater of its money back or its as-converted shareStandard; protects the downside only
ParticipatingIts money back plus its pro rata share of what is leftDouble dip; moves value from common at every exit
Capped participationParticipating up to a cap (for example 3x), or converts if betterA middle ground
Multiple (for example 2x)A multiple of its money before commonCommon in structured growth deals

Seniority sets the order: stacked (the latest round is paid first) or pari passu (paid together, pro rata). For non-participating preferred, the conversion breakpoint is preference ÷ ownership, the exit value above which converting beats taking the preference.

Anti-dilution

If a later round prices below the investor's price (a down round), anti-dilution adjusts its conversion price. Broad-based weighted average is the market standard:

CP₂ = CP₁ × (A + B) ÷ (A + C) A = shares outstanding before the new issue (fully diluted) B = shares the new money would buy at the old price (CP₁) C = shares actually issued in the new round

Full ratchet resets the conversion price to the new round's price. It is rare and very punitive to founders.

Other key terms

  • Pro rata rights: the right to keep your ownership in future rounds.
  • Protective provisions: preferred-holder vetoes over new senior securities, a sale, debt and similar actions.
  • Board composition: who controls decisions.
  • Drag-along: lets a majority force minority holders into a sale.
  • Information rights: what reporting you receive, and when.
Worked example · Cobalt Ledger

Option pool shuffle. The headline pre-money is $160M on 40.0M FD shares, including 1.6M unallocated options. Investors want a 10% post-money pool, created pre-money.

  • New pool shares needed: 3.89M, so the pool reaches 5.49M, which is 10% of 54.86M post-money shares.
  • Price per share: $160M ÷ 43.89M = $3.646, versus $4.00 with no shuffle.
  • Effective pre-money for existing holders: 40.0M × $3.646 = $145.8M. The shuffle costs existing holders about $14.2M of value.
  • The investor still owns 20.0%.

If the preference were fully participating, the investor would take $40M + 20% × $110M = $62M at a $150M exit, $22M more taken from common.

Down-round anti-dilution. Suppose a Series C raises $20M at $2.50 a share (8.0M new shares) on 54.86M FD shares. Broad-based weighted average: B = $20M ÷ $3.646 = 5.49M shares. CP₂ = $3.646 × (54.86 + 5.49) ÷ (54.86 + 8.0) = $3.50. Full ratchet would cut it to $2.50.

Practice check

Five questions

Question 1 of 5
$10M of 1x non-participating preferred owns 25% as converted. The company sells for $30M. What does the investor receive?
As converted it would get $7.5M, so it takes the $10M preference.
Question 2 of 5
Same facts, but the preferred is fully participating. What does the investor receive?
$10M preference, plus 25% of the remaining $20M ($5M), for $15M in total.
Question 3 of 5
What does the option pool shuffle do?
New pool shares are issued before the round, so only existing holders bear them.
Question 4 of 5
A $2M post-money SAFE has a $10M cap. What does it own at conversion, before the priced round's new money?
$2M ÷ $10M = 20%. The priced round then dilutes it like everyone else.
Question 5 of 5
Compared with full ratchet, broad-based weighted average anti-dilution is:
Full ratchet resets to the new price regardless of round size; weighted average blends the old and new prices.

AI watch-outs for this topic

Where AI output goes wrong here

  • Fully diluted share counts that silently drop the unallocated pool, warrants or SAFEs.
  • Treating a post-money SAFE like a pre-money SAFE, or the reverse.
  • Waterfalls that let non-participating preferred both take the preference and convert.
  • Ignoring seniority when there are several series.
  • Anti-dilution computed on basic instead of fully diluted shares (the "broad" in broad-based).
