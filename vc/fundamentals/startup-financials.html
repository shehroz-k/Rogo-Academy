Startup Financials Refresher

ARR vs. revenue vs. billings, deferred revenue, burn and runway — the accounting under every deck.

Teach

The three statements, startup edition

Income statement. Revenue, less cost of revenue (hosting, third-party software in the product, customer support and onboarding, payment processing), gives gross profit. Below that sit sales and marketing, R&D and G&A. Most venture-backed companies run an operating loss by design.

Balance sheet. Cash is the asset that matters most. Deferred revenue is a liability: cash collected for service not yet delivered. Accounts receivable is revenue earned but not yet collected.

Cash flow statement. Start with net loss, add back non-cash costs such as stock-based compensation, then adjust for working capital. Annual upfront billing makes operating cash flow look better than net loss when the company is growing.

Bookings, billings, revenue and ARR

MetricWhat it measuresGAAP?
Bookings (TCV / ACV)Contract value signed in the period; TCV is the whole contract, ACV is the annualized valueNo
BillingsWhat was invoiced in the period: revenue plus the change in deferred revenueDerived
RevenueWhat was earned and recognized in the period, ratably over the service termYes
ARRAnnualized value of recurring contracts live at a point in timeNo (operating metric)

Example: on January 1 a customer signs a three-year contract at $100K a year, billed annually upfront. TCV booking is $300K and ACV is $100K. Year-one billings are $100K on day one. Revenue is about $8.3K a month. ARR is $100K from go-live.

Definitions varyARR is not GAAP, and companies define it differently. Ask whether it includes usage or overage fees, professional services, contracts signed but not yet live, or multi-year ramps at their final-year value.

Deferred revenue mechanics

Bill $120K upfront → Cash +120 Deferred revenue +120 Each month → Revenue +10 Deferred revenue −10 After 3 months → Revenue recognized 30, deferred revenue 90

Burn and runway

Gross burn = total cash spent in the period Net burn = cash out − cash in (= decrease in cash before financing) Runway = cash ÷ monthly net burn

Simple runway assumes burn stays flat. Real runway depends on revenue growth, hiring plans and gross margin. A company is default alive if, on current trajectory, it reaches breakeven before cash runs out.

Things that make losses and cash diverge

  • Stock-based compensation is non-cash, but it is a real cost paid in dilution.
  • Capitalized software moves development spend out of the income statement into investing cash flow. Check whether the company's "burn" includes it.
  • EBITDA is not cash, and neither is "adjusted EBITDA" with SBC added back.

Gross margin quality

Software gross margins are usually high, but they fall when revenue mixes in services, when support and onboarding sit in cost of revenue, or when the product carries heavy usage costs such as third-party data or AI inference. Ask what sits in cost of revenue before comparing margins across companies.

Practice check

Five questions

Question 1 of 5
A customer signs a $120K annual contract billed upfront on January 1. At March 31, what are recognized revenue and deferred revenue?
Revenue is recognized ratably at $10K a month. Three months recognized means $30K; the remaining $90K is still owed in service.
Question 2 of 5
A growing company reports a $10M net loss but only $6M of operating cash outflow. What is the most likely explanation?
Prepaid annual contracts bring cash in ahead of revenue, and SBC is non-cash. Both make operating cash flow better than net loss.
Question 3 of 5
Cobalt Ledger has $12.0M of ARR but $9.1M of LTM revenue. Why?
For a fast-growing company, end-of-period ARR runs well ahead of trailing revenue.
Question 4 of 5
Cash is $22M and monthly net burn is $1.4M. What is simple runway?
22 ÷ 1.4 = 15.7 months. A company typically starts raising six to nine months before the zero-cash date.
Question 5 of 5
Which cost is usually NOT in a software company's cost of revenue?
Sales commissions are a customer acquisition cost and sit in sales and marketing.

AI watch-outs for this topic

Where AI output goes wrong here

  • Treating ARR, bookings and revenue as interchangeable. Ask for the metric, its definition and the period.
  • Computing runway on gross burn or on a single month that is not representative.
  • Missing capitalized software in burn, which flatters burn and EBITDA.
  • Comparing gross margins without checking what sits in cost of revenue.
  • Treating adjusted EBITDA with SBC added back as cash generation.
